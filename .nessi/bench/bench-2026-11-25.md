# KB vs NESSi re-evaluation: time and instruction counts (2026-11-25)

Measured 2026-11-25 23:39 to 2026-11-26 00:41 (Asia/Shanghai) on a
loaded machine (load average 6-12 from unrelated jobs; 18 logical
CPUs). Code: `3d7f79f` (`src/`, `Cargo.toml` and `examples/` unchanged
since `95135ce`), KB binaries built at 23:38 with
`cargo build --release --examples` from the clean tree at that commit.
The repository moved on during the measurement (weight-module
renames and doc edits up to `5e41a08`, plus uncommitted doc edits in
four `src/` files); none of that is in the measured binaries.
NESSi side as before: the bench programs of `build.sh` (`-O3`) against
the Release build of `libcntr` in `.nessi/ppsc-port/nessi-install`
(`-O3 -DNDEBUG`). Neither side passes `-march`, so both are SSE2 only
(no AVX, no OpenMP) and retired-instruction counts compare like with
like.

Throughout, a ratio > 1 means KB is slower / executes more.

## What the measurement itself showed

* **Unpinned runs migrate across core types.** A single unpinned
  `bench_convolution` run made 33-168 `cpu-migrations`, a NESSi run
  72-115. P3 times are therefore good to about +-20-30 % and do not
  reproduce from day to day: with identical binaries and instruction
  counts, KB `dyson d=2` printed 3.10 s on 2026-11-24 19:04 and 5.10 s
  on 2026-11-26 00:40, while `conv d=1` went from 0.37 s to 0.21 s.
* **Instructions are deterministic, cycles are not.** P1 and the two
  P2 sweeps agree on every instruction count to four digits. Cycles
  drift with load: NESSi `conv d=2` IPC came out as 4.85, 5.15 and 4.19
  in the three sweeps of this session.
* **Cycles do not convert to time across the two programs.** On the
  same pinned core the effective clock (cycles / CPU time) of the KB
  conv and dyson runs is 3.4-3.9 GHz against 2.9-3.5 GHz for NESSi,
  whose higher IPC comes with a lower clock. The cause was not
  investigated (AVX frequency licensing is excluded, both sides are
  SSE2). Time therefore has to be measured, which is why P2 reads
  `task-clock` from the same runs.
* **perf counts the whole process.** Both convolution benches run
  one untimed warm-up and one timed run, so P1/P2 conv numbers cover
  about twice the work of the printed time (equally on both sides).

## Corrections to the 2026-11-24 record

* The sentence "At d >= 2 every kernel is now at or faster than NESSi"
  in `bench-2026-11-24.md` came from the unpinned 19:04 sweep and is
  not supported by the pinned data: at d=2 and d=4 the convolution is a
  tie and VIE2 is 13-21 % slower. It is superseded by the P2 table
  above.
* The `-27 %` / `-16 %` conv cycle figures in `bench-2026-11-24.md` are
  KB before versus KB after; they must not be read as KB versus NESSi
  (the pinned KB/NESSi cycle ratio for conv is 1.18x / 1.12x at d=2/4,
  P2).

## Reading

* Instructions: KB needs 36-37 % fewer for the convolution at d=2/4 and
  15-49 % fewer for Dyson at d=1/2/4 and 18 % fewer for `vie2 d=1`,
  but 5-12 % more for VIE2 at d=2/4,
  6 % more for `conv d=1`, and 2-9 % more at d=8.
* Time (pinned): KB is faster for Dyson at d=2/4 (12-18 %) and slower
  for VIE2 at d=2/4 (13-21 %); the convolution at d=2/4 is a tie and
  `conv d=1` is 32 % slower. `dyson d=1` is a tie, `vie2 d=1` 10 %
  faster. d=8 favours KB for conv and vie2 within single-sample error.
* The instruction lead does not become a time lead where KB's IPC is
  about half of NESSi's (conv at d=2/4: 2.5-2.8 against 4.6-4.9).
