| vie2 d=4 | 1.29e11 | 1.16e11 | 1.12x | 2.58 | 3.38 |

Reading:

* KB executes far FEWER instructions wherever the blocks are matrix
  sized: 50-54% of NESSi for dyson d=2/d=4 and 63-66% for conv
  d=2/d=4, 85-87% at d=1 for the dyson/vie2 kernels. The wall-time
  gap is therefore NOT instruction count: it is IPC. KB runs at
  2.0-2.6 instructions per cycle where NESSi's Eigen kernels run at
  4.6-5.4. The remaining work is code generation quality inside
  `mul_acc`/block loops (bounds checks, vectorization, dependency
  chains), not algorithmic.
* dyson d=8 is the case where KB also wins on IPC (4.55 vs 4.04)
  with equal instruction counts -> the 0.96x wall-time win.
* conv d=1 is the only kernel where KB executes more instructions
  (1.06x); its 1.65x wall-time ratio from the timed sweep is
  measurement noise: the perf cycles ratio is 1.22x. Instruction
  counts are the load-independent number to trust.
* vie2 is the only family where KB does not have the instruction
  advantage (1.04-1.12x at d=2/d=4) AND has lower IPC (2.0-2.6 vs
  2.9-3.4) -> consistently the weakest kernel, consistent with the
  timed sweep.

## IPC optimization round (2026-11-24, evening)

Target: the LLC-miss gap (dyson d=2 nt=400: 8.06e6 vs NESSi 9.6e5,
8.4x). Address pattern found: `incr_lmix_rmix_buf` walks the
A^rangle column (j fixed, m varying) while lmix is stored
(tstp, tau) row-major, so every step of the inner m loop jumps a
6.4KB stride. The outer/inner loops were swapped (m outer, j inner)
with the per-j right-hand blocks precomputed; each buffer still
accumulates in ascending j with identical products, so the
arithmetic (and every reference test) is unchanged. Applied to the
d = 1, 2 and 4 branches (d = 8 and the generic path were already
row-major or are not exercised by the benchmarks).

A first attempt that flattened `gles`/`buf` from `[Vec<Complex>]` to
one contiguous `Vec<Complex>` was measured and REVERTED: cycles
+12% (dyson d=2 nt=400), LLC misses +22% — the pointer-chasing
buffers are small enough (a few KB) to stay in L1/L2, and the
`chunks_exact_mut` walk generated worse code than the `iter_mut`
over `Vec` pointers.

## C/D rounds: convolution kernels (2026-11-24 18:04-19:04)

Same principle continued into the Type-1 convolution. `A^rangle(m, j)`
in `src/convolution/less.rs::conv_lmix_rmix_kernel` walked the lmix rows
with the `(ntau+1)*nel` stride in m; the d=2 and d=4 specializations now
precompute `bj[j]`/`alpha[j]` and walk m outer, j inner (each target
block still accumulates ascending j with the same products).

| kernel | cycles before | after | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| conv d=2 (nt=400) | 1.775e10 | 1.29e10 | -27% |
| conv d=4 (nt=400) | 6.745e10 | 5.65e10 | -16% |

Rejected in the same round: a d=2 specialization of
`conv_ret_less_col` (mul_acc_c -> mul_acc_c2, unchecked blocks, sliced
target column). Its retired instructions dropped 12% but cycles rose
15% (1.44e10 -> 1.65e10): the generic row-axpy `mul_acc_c`
auto-vectorizes for n=2 while the unrolled `mul_acc_c2` leaf does not.
Reverted to the generic path (`pre-d-ret-less-col-d2` checkpoint);
this reproduces the note already in `linalg/mul.rs` from the earlier
A/B round.
