# KB vs NESSi efficiency comparison (2026-11-17, idle machine)

Machine: Intel Core Ultra 5 125H, load average ~1-3 during the whole
window (no competing jobs). Protocol: interleaved same-window rounds,
per-entry medians; KB `--release` (opt-level 3, thin LTO, 1 codegen
unit), NESSi `-O3` from `.nessi/nessi-2.0.0` + prebuilt libcntr.

Reproduce with:

    cargo build --release --examples
    ./.nessi/bench/build.sh
    python3 .nessi/bench/compare_e2e.py
    python3 .nessi/bench/compare_kernels.py

## Comparison with the 2026-11-16 record

The overall pattern is fully reproduced: d=1 is the worst case
(~6.3-8.7x, NESSi has scalar fast paths), vie2 is the weakest family
(2.8-3.4x at d>=2), conv/dyson sit in the 1.2-1.6x band at d>=2, and
the end-to-end suite leads KB by 2.2-2.8x per entry.

Two deltas against 2026-11-16: vie2 d=4 improved slightly (3.2x ->
2.78x), and dyson d=8 does NOT reproduce the recorded 0.95x crossover
(today 1.21x: 113.5s vs 93.8s). On the idle machine NESSi keeps a
clear lead at d=8; the earlier 0.95x should be treated as a
load-window artifact.

## Conclusions (optimization backlog)

1. `vie2_timestep` path: never optimized; 3.4x at d=2 and 8.7x at d=1.
2. d=1 scalar specialization: consistent ~6.5-8.7x across all three
   kernels; single-band DMFT is the direct beneficiary.
3. The 1.2-1.6x band at d>=2 is the structural cost of safe slices
   vs raw pointers + Eigen; lower yield per effort than 1-2.
